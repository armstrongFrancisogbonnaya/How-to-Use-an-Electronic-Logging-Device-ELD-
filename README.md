# How-to-Use-an-Electronic-Logging-Device-ELD-
A driver’s guide to logging in, changing duty status, completing DVIRs and fixing common errors

Prepared by: Armstrong Francis Ogbonnaya

Contact: armstrongogbonnaya.official@gmail.com

Date: March 2025


1. About this guide

An Electronic Logging Device, or ELD, records when a commercial vehicle is moving and what the driver is doing
while on the job. It connects to the engine, reads speed, engine hours and mileage, picks up a GPS position, and
builds your Record of Duty Status (RODS) without you having to draw a single line on a paper log.
This guide follows an ordinary working day: signing in, setting your duty status, keeping the record accurate,
inspecting the vehicle, and dealing with problems when they appear. The rules mentioned come from the FMCSA
ELD regulation (49 CFR Part 395, Subpart B) and the inspection requirements in 49 CFR 396.11 and 396.13. Menu
names and button positions differ from one ELD brand to another, so keep your manufacturer’s manual close by and
use this guide as the general walk-through.

2. Before you start

Spend two minutes on the following before you roll out. It saves a lot of trouble later.

• Your own login details. Your username and password come from your carrier. They are personal to you.

• A working connection. Check that the ELD (or the tablet and app) is powered, mounted and connected to the
truck.

• The required papers in the cab. The ELD user manual, the sheet explaining how to send logs to an inspector,
the sheet explaining how to report a malfunction, and at least eight days of blank paper log sheets.

• Your hours cycle. Know whether your carrier runs a 60-hour/7-day or 70-hour/8-day schedule. The ELD uses it
to calculate your remaining time.

3. Driver login

Every driver has a separate account. The ELD uses it to decide who was behind the wheel, so never sign in with
someone else’s details, and never let anyone else use yours.

a. Stop first. Switch on the ignition and wait for the ELD to finish starting up. Log in only while the vehicle is
stationary.

b. Enter your credentials. Type your own username and password, then tap Log In. If the screen reports an error,
check for a typing slip before you try again. Repeated wrong attempts can lock the account.

c. Select your vehicle. Choose the truck number if the device asks for it, and add your trailer number(s) and
shipping document number when prompted.

d. Review what is waiting for you. At sign-in the ELD may show proposed edits from your carrier or driving that
was recorded without a driver. Open each item and either accept or reject it.

e. Check your starting status and clocks. Confirm the status shown is correct and glance at your remaining
driving time.

f. Team driving. The second driver signs in through the Co-Driver option on the same unit. Each of you then
controls your own duty status.

If you drive before logging in, the ELD stores that movement as unidentified driving. When you next sign in, claim any of it
that was yours. If it was not, leave it for the carrier to deal with. To end a session, set your status first (usually Off Duty), then
choose Log Out.

5. Updating your duty status

Changing status takes only a few taps. The skill is remembering to do it at the right moment.

a. Open the Status or Duty Status screen from the main menu.

b. Select the new status, for example On-Duty (Not Driving) when you arrive at a shipper.

c. Add a short remark if the screen asks for one, such as “loading at dock” or “fueling”. Keep it factual.

d. Confirm, then check that the colour bar or status banner shows the change before you walk away.

What the ELD does by itself

Once the truck passes 5 mph the ELD switches you to Driving. When it has been stopped for five minutes, the device
asks whether you are still driving. Answer within one minute. If you do not, it moves you to On-Duty (Not Driving),
which can quietly eat into your 14-hour window.

Special categories

• Personal Conveyance: using the truck for personal reasons while off duty, for example driving to a restaurant or
a hotel after being released. It must not move the load or bring you closer to the next stop.

• Yard Moves: moving the vehicle inside a private yard or terminal. This is logged as On-Duty, not Driving, and your
carrier must allow it.

Fixing mistakes and certifying your day

If you picked the wrong status, use Edit on the log, make the correction and write a clear note explaining why. The
original entry is kept; your change sits on top of it. Driving time recorded by the ELD cannot be deleted. 

When something is wrong with it, for instance a mechanic moved your truck, annotate it and tell your carrier. At the end of
each day, read through the whole log and tap Certify. That signature confirms the entries are true.

Roadside inspection. Keep the log current and your credentials ready. The officer may ask to see the screen or request a
transfer by the method your device supports (Bluetooth, web service, email or USB). Follow the instruction sheet kept in the
cab.


6. Driver Vehicle Inspection Reports (DVIRs)
   
A DVIR is a written record of the vehicle’s condition. It is separate from your hours of service, but many ELD apps
include a DVIR screen so the whole workflow stays in one place. You should complete one at the end of each day’s
work (post-trip), and most carriers also expect a pre-trip check before the first move of the day.

a. Open the Inspection or DVIR menu and select the truck and any trailer you are pulling.

b. Walk around the vehicle and check the items listed by regulation: service brakes and trailer brake connections,

parking brake, steering, lights and reflectors, tires, wheels and rims, horn, windshield wipers, mirrors, coupling
devices and emergency equipment.
c. Mark any defect and describe it in plain words (“left rear tire worn on inner edge” tells the shop more than “tire
bad”). Add a photo if the app supports it.

d. If nothing is wrong, mark the vehicle as satisfactory. Many carriers still want a report in this case, so follow
company policy.

e. Sign and submit the report. Your carrier is required to keep it for three months.
Before you drive, look at the previous driver’s DVIR. If defects were reported, check that a mechanic has certified them
repaired or that repair was not needed, then sign. Do not take a vehicle out with a defect that could affect safe operation until
it has been fixed.

If the ELD stops working altogether

• Write down what happened and tell your carrier in writing within 24 hours.

• Recreate your record for the current day and the previous seven days on paper logs, unless the ELD data was
already retrieved.

• Keep using paper logs until the device is repaired or replaced. Carriers have eight days to do this.
